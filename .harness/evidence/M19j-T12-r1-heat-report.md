Installation costs for typical UK heating systems in 2026 vary by type, with standard gas combi swaps ranging from £1,800 to £3,500 [1]. System boilers typically cost between £2,500 and £4,000 to install, while conventional or regular gas boilers range from £2,500 to £4,200 [1]. Complex projects involving boiler conversion or relocation can exceed £3,500 to over £5,500 [1]. Additionally, a required system power flush may add an extra £300 to £600 to gas boiler installation costs [1]. In contrast, the government Boiler Upgrade Scheme offers a £7,500 grant for eligible households installing heat pumps, rising to £9,000 for homes currently heated by oil or LPG [2]. This enhanced grant of £9,000 remains available for qualifying installations until March 2027 [2].

Regarding running costs and unit prices as of October 2026, natural gas unit prices have risen by 8.7%, whereas electricity unit rates have increased by only 0.2% [4]. Despite the sharp rise in gas prices, average dual-fuel energy bills are projected to increase by approximately 4% starting October 1, 2026 [4]. While the provided notes do not supply a specific kWh quantity for a typical home's annual usage, the price differential indicates that electricity rates remain relatively stable compared to the recent sharp increases in natural gas costs [4].

In terms of performance during cold UK winters, modern air source heat pumps maintain significant efficiency, retaining a Coefficient of Performance (CoP) above 2.0 even at temperatures as low as -10°C [5]. Although efficiency drops when temperatures fall, shifting from a Seasonal Performance Factor (SPF) of 3.5 to 2.5 results in a 40% rise in electricity consumption for the same heat output, yet this impact on annual bills is modest due to the rarity of extreme cold days [5]. Supplementary electric heating is rarely required, with built-in auxiliary heaters activating only if the system drops below the setpoint temperature for just a few hours per year [5]. User satisfaction data reflects this reliability, with 89% of heat pump owners reporting satisfaction with their systems during cold snaps compared to 78% of gas boiler users [6]. Furthermore, 85% of heat pump owners felt their homes were kept warm effectively during cold weather, outperforming the 80% satisfaction rate reported by gas boiler users [6].
