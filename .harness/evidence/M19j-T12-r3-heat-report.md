As of October 2026, the average installation cost for an air-to-water heat pump in the UK is between £8,000 and £16,500, whereas a typical gas boiler replacement costs between £2,200 and £4,500 [1][2]. Labour constitutes a significant portion of the total heat pump installation cost, accounting for between £3,500 and £6,000 of the total [1]. Specific government grants are available to offset these costs; the Energy Company Obligation (ECO4) scheme offers funding covering up to 100% of installation costs for low-income households in England until December 2026, while the Boiler Upgrade Scheme provides £2,500 for air-to-air heat pump installations [1][2]. Additionally, these figures include VAT, which is currently 0% on heat pump installations until March 2027, further reducing the net cost [1]. Regarding running costs, modern air-source heat pumps operate with a Coefficient of Performance (COP) of 3.0 to 3.5 at typical UK winter temperatures around 4°C, delivering performance of 300-350% compared to gas boilers during mild conditions [6]. Even during colder periods, certified units maintain high efficiency; most models keep a COP of 2.5 or higher even at -5°C and remain above 2.5 for most MCS-certified models at -2°C [6]. However, performance metrics must account for varying ambient temperatures, as efficiency can drop significantly by 50% during winter design conditions compared to mild conditions, and units may spend 5–10% of run time in energy-intensive defrost cycles rather than heating [5].
