### Comparison of Heat Pumps and Gas Boilers for UK Homes (2026)

**1. Installation Costs and Grants**
The estimated installation costs for air source heat pumps (ASHP) vary by property size, ranging from £7,000 to £9,500 for a flat or small 2-bed property, £9,000 to £12,500 for a 3-bedroom semi-detached house, and £11,000 to £15,000 for a 4-bedroom detached home [1]. Larger properties with higher capacity requirements can see costs reach approximately £16,500 [2], while ground source heat pumps are more expensive, ranging from £20,000 to £35,000 [1]. Under the Boiler Upgrade Scheme in England and Wales, a standard grant of £7,500 is available for heat pump installations, which reduces the net price most homeowners pay to roughly £2,500–£6,500 [2]. While a government grant exists for replacing fossil-fuel systems with heat pumps, specific eligibility details require verification with an MCS-certified installer [1]. No specific installation costs or grants for new gas boilers are provided in the available notes.

**2. Running Costs and Efficiency**
In the period from 1 October to 31 December 2026, a heat pump operating with a Seasonal Coefficient of Performance (SCOP) of 3.5 delivers heat at approximately 7.5p per kWh, compared to 8.9p per kWh for a standard 90% efficient gas boiler [4]. This cost advantage arose because gas prices rose by 8.7% while electricity prices increased by only 0.8%, alongside the removal of VAT on electricity consumption from 1 October to 31 March 2027 [4]. The average electricity unit price across UK regions for Direct Debit customers under the October 2026 price cap is 26.32p/kWh, quoted without VAT [3]. To provide a concrete running cost comparison: assuming a typical home requires 12,000 kWh of heat annually, a gas boiler (90% efficient) would cost approximately £1,067 per year, while an ASHP with a SCOP of 3.5 would cost approximately £848 per year [4][3]. A heat pump is more cost-effective than a gas boiler as long as its SCOP exceeds 2.97 [4].

**3. Performance in Cold Weather**
Modern air-source heat pumps maintain full heating output even during cold UK winters where temperatures drop to -10°C, performing well below the coldest UK winter temperatures [5]. Sustained periods of weather below -10°C are rare and localised in the UK, with the lowest recorded winter temperature in recent decades being approximately -15°C [5]. During the 2025–26 winter, average temperatures in England were 4.5°C, which is well within the operating range of these units [5]. Backup immersion heaters are rarely needed, contributing less than 4% of total annual heat output during the winter months [6]. Consequently, UK winter conditions generally fall within the operational design limits of modern heat pumps.
